# Tomato Late Blight (Phytophthora)

Crop: tomato  
Class id: Tomato___Late_blight

## What it is

Late blight is a fast-spreading disease of tomato (and potato). Irregular water-soaked or grey-green patches appear on leaves, often with white mould on the underside in humid weather. Stems can blacken. Fruit may get firm brown blotches. Cool nights, wet leaves, and cloudy weather favour outbreaks. It can destroy a crop in a few days.

## Treatment options

- Remove and destroy infected plants or badly affected shoots as soon as you see them. Do not leave them in the field.
- Avoid overhead watering. Reduce leaf wetness.
- There is no household cure that reliably stops an outbreak. A local extension officer may recommend a registered protectant product for tomato late blight if the disease is just starting. Do not invent a chemical name, dose, or spray schedule. Follow only labelled, locally registered products.

## Prevention

- Start with healthy seedlings. Do not plant tomato next to blighted potato.
- Stake plants and space them so air can move through the canopy.
- Scout twice a week in the rainy season.
- Destroy volunteer tomato and potato plants.
- After a severe outbreak, discuss resistant varieties with your extension office or input dealer who stocks certified seed.
