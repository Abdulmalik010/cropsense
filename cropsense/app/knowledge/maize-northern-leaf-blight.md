# Maize Northern Leaf Blight

Crop: maize  
Class id: Maize___Northern_Leaf_Blight

## What it is

Northern leaf blight is a fungal disease of maize. Long, cigar-shaped grey-green to tan lesions run between leaf veins. In wet weather the lesions expand and leaves can die early, which reduces grain fill. Moderate temperatures and long leaf wetness favour it.

## Treatment options

- There is no practical field spray that smallholders should mix from memory.
- Remove badly blighted leaves only if the infestation is light and plants are few; in a full field, focus on variety and residue management.
- A local extension officer may advise a registered maize fungicide in high-value seed plots. Do not guess product names or rates. Most grain fields are managed with resistant varieties and rotation, not routine spraying.

## Prevention

- Plant maize varieties recommended locally for leaf-blight tolerance if available.
- Rotate maize with legumes or other non-maize crops where possible.
- Bury or remove old maize stalks that carry the fungus.
- Avoid very late planting into a long wet spell if you have a choice of date.
- Do not overcrowd plants; follow recommended spacing.
