# Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria)

Crop: tomato  
Class id: Tomato___Early_blight

## What it is

Early blight is a fungal disease common on tomato leaves, stems, and sometimes fruit. It often starts on older leaves as brown spots with concentric rings that look like a target. Leaves may yellow around the spots and drop. Warm weather and frequent wetting of leaves (rain, overhead watering, heavy dew) favour the disease.

## Treatment options

- Remove and destroy badly spotted lower leaves. Do not compost diseased material if you can burn or bury it away from the garden.
- Keep water off the leaves: water the soil at the base of the plant in the morning.
- Improve airflow by staking plants and avoiding dense foliage.
- A local extension officer may recommend a fungicide that is registered for tomato early blight in your state. Do not guess a product name or mix rate. If a product is advised, follow the label for dose, interval, and days-to-harvest.

## Prevention

- Use certified or healthy seedlings. Avoid plants that already show spots.
- Rotate tomato with crops that are not tomato, potato, or pepper for at least one season where land allows.
- Mulch the soil to reduce splash of fungal spores from soil onto leaves.
- Do not work in the plot when leaves are wet.
- Remove crop residues after harvest.
