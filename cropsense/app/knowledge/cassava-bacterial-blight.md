# Cassava Bacterial Blight

Crop: cassava  
Class id: Cassava___bacterial_blight

## What it is

Cassava bacterial blight is caused by bacteria. Angular water-soaked leaf spots can turn brown, leaves wilt, and shoot tips may die back (“dieback”). In wet weather, a sticky ooze may appear on stems. Infected cuttings and rain splash spread the disease. It is worse in the rains and on susceptible varieties.

## Treatment options

- Cut out and burn or bury severely affected shoots. Disinfect the knife with heat or a fresh bleach solution if an extension officer shows you a safe mix; do not invent a chemical recipe.
- Do not use stems from blighted plants as new cuttings.
- Copper or other bactericides are not a routine smallholder solution unless a local officer names a product registered for cassava and explains how to use it. CropSense does not give a dose.

## Prevention

- Plant healthy cuttings from blight-free plots.
- Avoid working in the field when plants are dripping wet.
- Space plants so air can dry the canopy.
- Ask about locally released tolerant varieties.
